# Publication and marketing

Ideas collected during the concept phase. None of it is decided — this is here
so it does not get lost.

## Positioning

The point is **not** "print bingo cards" (there are hundreds of those) but:
**every guest shouts "bingo!" at the same instant**.

That only works with a fixed, predetermined draw order. And that is exactly
where the distinctive part lies — not in the cards, but in the control over the
draw.

It is explicitly not about favouring anyone, and not about money. Nobody is put
at a disadvantage; there is no injured party. The prize is something that was
there for everyone anyway.

### The name: Bingo Coup

Decided after a long search. "Coup" means a stroke pulled off — positively
loaded without being tame, and it works in German, English and French. One name
for every market.

The decisive thought behind it: the name does not have to reassure the
**guests**, it has to speak to the **host**. With the white-label option the
guests never see it. The host, on the other hand, is not a consumer buying a
party game — they are pulling something off. "I pulled off a coup at my
grandparents' party" is a sentence somebody tells unprompted, and that is the
best marketing there is.

Rejected candidates and why:

| Name | Reason |
|---|---|
| BingoFaker | sounds like fraud; stays as the project's origin |
| Konfettibingo / Confetti Bingo | **withconfetti.com** actively sells virtual bingo for company events — same category, same segment |
| Surprise Bingo | generic, hard to protect, and it announces the surprise |
| Jubelbingo, Festbingo, Tafelbingo | too tame; Festbingo is also purely descriptive and so barely protectable as a mark |
| Shock Bingo | "Schock" carries a medical sense in German — unfortunate with eighty-year-old guests |
| Rigged Bingo | consistent and good for Hacker News, but unintelligible in the German market |

**Still open: the trade mark registers have not been checked.** DPMA and EUIPO
could not be queried automatically. Research it before filing anything
([TMview](https://www.tmdn.org/tmview/) covers both); the relevant Nice classes
are 28 (games), 41 (entertainment) and 9 (software).

## Use cases

Anywhere a shared moment for a group is the point:

| Occasion | What makes it work |
|---|---|
| Golden or silver wedding | the project's original use case |
| A wedding | the guest favour as the "prize" for everyone |
| A children's birthday | no losers, no tears — the strongest argument for parents |
| A company or team event | "we all win together" — commercially the most interesting market |
| A Christmas party | everybody unwraps their secret-santa gift at the same moment |
| Conference, trade fair, customer event | activating an audience, a goodie for everyone |
| Care homes, parish fairs, class reunions | bingo is already played there |
| As a transition in the running order | the "prize" is the announcement: the buffet is open, the cake is coming, the speech begins |

## The prize

The prize is not a competitive win but the trigger for a shared moment. The best
version: **the wrapped parcel sits on every place all evening** and must not be
opened. Guests puzzle over it during dinner — then everybody wins at once and
unwraps at the same moment.

Other versions: everybody raises a glass together, everybody lights their
sparkler, a group photo (they are all standing up anyway), opening the buffet.

## Variations on the drama

- **Everybody at once** — the standard
- **A shared final number**: every card already has four marks in its winning
  line and is set off by the same number. The tension builds visibly across two
  or three minutes rather than arriving out of nowhere. Dramatically the
  strongest version — and, as it turns out, unavoidable.
- **The wave**: table by table, a second apart, across the room
- **Two camps**: the bride's family on draw 22, the groom's on 23
- **Everybody but one**: the room wins together, one person a draw later on
  their own — and gets the applause for it
- **The false winner**: somebody calls bingo early, it was a mistake; later
  everybody wins

## Channels

Sorted by expected effect:

### 1. Video (TikTok, Reels, YouTube Shorts)
The effect is the content. "80 people shout bingo at the same time" needs no
explanation — you can see it. It carries itself.

### 2. Google / SEO
Search terms with clear intent and weak competition: `Bingo Goldene Hochzeit`,
`Hochzeitsspiele Bingo Vorlage`, `Bingo alle gewinnen`, `Spiel ohne Verlierer
Kindergeburtstag`, `Hochzeitsspiel alle Gäste`. A landing page plus two or three
guide articles.

### 3. Pinterest
In German-speaking countries the strongest channel for wedding decoration and
games. A pretty card layout is the product there.

### 4. Hacker News / r/programming / r/math
The hook is not the product but the combinatorial problem: *how many different
cards can be built that all win on exactly draw k — and none earlier?* With 75
numbers and 25 draws that is a real question, not a rhetorical one. A technical
article about it is better marketing than any product post.

### 5. Wedding forums, blogs, Facebook groups
weddingstyle, hochzeitsplaza, wedding-planning groups.

### 6. Reddit in general
Weaker than it feels: German-speaking wedding planning barely happens there, and
self-promotion gets removed from most subs. If at all, then as an honest story
in r/de or r/Hochzeit — "I built a bingo for my grandparents' golden wedding
where everybody wins at the same time" — with the code and without a sales
pitch.
